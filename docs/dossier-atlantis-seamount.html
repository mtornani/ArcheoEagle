Forensic dossier · open data, reproducible

An island, yes. Twelve thousand years ago, no.

Heezen et al. dredged limestone from the Atlantis Seamount in 1969, saw subaerial lithification, and dated a cobble to 12,000 ± 900 yr by radiocarbon. The observation was right. The clock was the wrong one — and a better clock has been available for decades.

What we are not doing

This is not a debunk. Three parts of the underlying case survive contact with the data, and one of them is a measurement most sceptics have never read.

  • ✓

    Continental material does exist under the mid-Atlantic

    Palaeozoic (~330 Ma) and Proterozoic (~1600 Ma) zircons were recovered from gabbros near the Kane Fracture Zone and published in Nature in 1998. Whatever it means, it is real and measured.

  • ✓

    The Atlantis Seamount really was an island

    Its summit is a flat plain at −268 m. Great Meteor, in the same chain, has a twin at −274 m. Two flat tops within 6 m of each other is the signature of guyots: wave-cut platforms planed at sea level, then drowned together.

  • ✓

    Heezen read the rock correctly

    Subaerial lithification in that limestone is exactly what a drowned wave-cut platform should show. The geological interpretation was sound.

The measurement

GMRT GridServer, no key, anyone can repeat it. A grid of 11.5 million soundings over the seamount returns −268.0 m at 34.1293°N, 30.2430°W. Not one cell in the grid rises above −265 m. A published secondary figure gives 265 m; the two agree within three metres.

The clock Heezen didn't have

Oceanic lithosphere sinks as it cools, on a law calibrated across every ocean basin. A guyot planed flat at sea level rides down with the plate beneath it — so the depth of its summit is a chronometer. The rate falls steeply with plate age, and that is the crux.

The crust under Great Meteor is 85 Ma, from magnetic anomaly 34. The Seewarte chain was built on the African plate over the New England hotspot between 26 and 10 Ma; Great Meteor basalts date to 16 and 11 Ma. Old plate barely moves — metres per million years.

The answer

A guyot is planed while its volcano is active or shortly after. Take the chain's own volcanic window as the planation window, and the implied subsidence rate follows directly:

Planed atImplied rate
26 Ma0.0103 mm / yr
16 Ma0.0168 mm / yr
11 Ma0.0244 mm / yr
10 Ma0.0268 mm / yr
12,000 yr — the 1969 claim16.9 mm / yr

The 12,000-year date is off by a factor of 631 to 1,641.

Not measured against rates borrowed from somewhere else — measured against the geology of this chain. The Atlantis Seamount was an island roughly 10 to 26 million years ago.

The likely culprit is specific and forgivable: radiocarbon on marine carbonate at ~12 ka is the worst case that method has. A few percent of modern carbon from recrystallisation makes a limestone of any age read as twelve thousand years. In 1969 there was no alternative. There is now.

Limits we declare

  • !

    Plate cooling alone at 85 Ma gives 65–170 m over 10–26 Myr, not 268 m. The balance comes from hotspot swell decay and volcanic loading flexure — both real, both expected on a hotspot edifice, neither computed here. These are orders of magnitude, not a date. For a thousand-fold gap they are enough.

  • !

    Earlier in this work we compared against Azores subsidence rates. That was wrong: the Azores are an active plateau on a triple junction; this chain is intraplate on old lithosphere. We corrected it rather than quietly dropping it.

  • !

    We could not read what Randall Carlson argues today, after the November 2025 expedition. Everything above addresses the 2019 episode and the 1969 paper behind it.

What we are asking for

Three things we cannot get, and you might

This dossier was built entirely from public data by two people with a laptop. That is its point and also its ceiling. Three things would move it, and none of them are ours to reach.

  1. A U–Th date on the dredged carbonate. Uranium–thorium does not suffer the contamination that wrecks radiocarbon in this window. One date on archived Lamont material would replace our order-of-magnitude estimate with a number. If the samples still exist, someone should run it.
  2. Cement petrography on the same limestone. Meteoric and marine cements are now distinguished by fabric and stable isotopes. That would settle the subaerial question properly instead of on the 1969 criterion of "state of lithification".
  3. Anything from the 2025 Azores expedition that bears on deposits or cores. We weighed the claims we could find. We would rather weigh the current ones.

If any of it contradicts what is above, we will say so in writing and change the file. That has happened five times in this project already.

How this was built

Every prediction in this project is committed to version control before the test runs, so the timestamp proves it came first. Positive controls are mandatory: a detector that cannot find a known target is not reporting on unknown ones. Five separate lines of investigation have been closed by their own evidence, including two the authors wanted to be true.

The rule this dossier came from: treat a proposition as a thesis with a source, test it clause by clause, and let each clause take its own verdict. Discarding it unread would have lost a real observation. Swallowing it whole would have carried a thousand-fold error.
